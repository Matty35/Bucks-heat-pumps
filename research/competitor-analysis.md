## 2. Competitor profiles

### AOS Heat Pumps — aosheatpumps.co.uk
- **Structure:** Hub-and-spoke: national/brand homepage plus county and district landing pages (`/buckinghamshire`, `/aylesbury-vale`), separate `/reviews` and `/servicing` pages. Heat-pump-only specialist positioning.
- **Trust signals:** "Award-winning" claims, ~38–40 years' experience messaging, dedicated testimonials page, maintenance plans (annual service ~£180, emergency cover plan). ⚠ MCS number and third-party review scores (Trustpilot etc.) were not visible in indexed data.
- **Content coverage:** Cites the £7,500 BUS grant on area pages. No indexed mention of the £9,000 oil/LPG uplift, air-to-air grants, planning/noise rules, or listed buildings.
- **Weaknesses:** Hertfordshire-first branding — the Bucks page is a templated satellite, not locally grounded. Servicing revenue focus dilutes the install funnel.
- **Location targeting:** Templated county/district landing pages across the East and South East. Broad but shallow — no village-level or housing-stock-specific content.

### Buckinghamshire Heating — buckinghamshireheating.com
- **Structure:** Boiler-first site (nav dominated by boiler installation, servicing, aircon); ASHP is a single `/air-source/` page framed as a service expansion. Town-level pages exist for *boilers* (High Wycombe, Amersham, Great Missenden) but not for heat pumps.
- **Trust signals:** Worcester Bosch Gold Accredited Partner, Gas Safe registered, up-to-10-year guarantee on Worcester boilers, positive testimonials. ⚠ No MCS accreditation evident — if they are not MCS certified they cannot process BUS grants themselves, a major competitive weakness despite the exact-match domain.
- **Content coverage:** Promotes Worcester Bosch hybrid (boiler + ASHP) systems. No indexed grant-uplift, air-to-air, planning, listed-building, or oil-running-cost content.
- **Weaknesses:** Heat pumps are clearly an add-on; the ASHP page is thin. Ranks #2 largely on the exact-match domain name, not content depth — very displaceable.
- **Location targeting:** County name in the brand/domain; town pages only for boiler services.

### Renewable Energy Hub — renewableenergyhub.co.uk (Q1 #3, Q2 #1)
- **Structure:** Standard directory format: intro copy about heat pumps in the county/town, list of member installers, quote-request form, links to generic educational guides.
- **Trust signals:** Aggregates installers' own accreditations; no first-party trust.
- **Content coverage:** Generic national heat-pump information; nothing localised beyond the place name; no 2026 grant-uplift specifics on indexed area pages.
- **Weaknesses:** Lead-gen middleman — visitors are sold as leads. Thin, boilerplate local pages. Beatable by any genuinely local page with real content.
- **Location targeting:** Programmatic place-name pages for every county/town in the UK.

### Green Mechanical Solutions — greenmechanical.co.uk
- **Structure:** Local trades site: homepage covering heat pumps/boilers/AC, dedicated `/heat-pumps` and `/air-source-heat-pumps/` pages plus a Buckingham-localised variant.
- **Trust signals:** Strong on paper: MCS certified (cert no. OFT-100856), OFTEC registered, HIES consumer code member, Mitsubishi Accredited Installer, Samsung Gen 6 qualified (7-year manufacturer warranty), ~20 years' ASHP experience, "largest local ASHP installer" claim.
- **Content coverage:** Explains the £7,500 BUS and that they handle the application end-to-end. No £9,000 uplift, air-to-air, planning/noise, listed-building, or oil-comparison content indexed.
- **Weaknesses:** No visible third-party review platform (no Trustpilot/Checkatrade presence surfaced) — accreditation-heavy but social-proof-light. Buckingham-centric; weak coverage of Aylesbury/High Wycombe terms.
- **Location targeting:** Buckingham town page; service-area statement (Bucks, Oxon, Northants).

### Heppelthwaite "The Red Van Plumbers" — redvanplumbers.co.uk
- **Structure:** Large plumbing/heating site; ASHP lives 3 levels deep (`/boiler-and-heating-services/renewable-energy/air-source-heat-pumps/`) with manufacturer sub-pages (e.g. Vaillant) and an extensive `/areas/` tree (`/areas/buckinghamshire/aylesbury/`, `/high-wycombe/`, `/amersham/`, `/hazlemere/`, `/flackwell-heath/`, plus Hertfordshire etc.).
- **Trust signals:** Established 1948 (Evans Group), 1,000+ five-star reviews claimed, Checkatrade profile, MCS-certified engineers, Vaillant partner, survey/heat-loss-calculation process described. Headline workmanship guarantee is only 30 days — weak versus 7-year warranties elsewhere.
- **Content coverage:** Solid process content (survey → heat-loss → design → install). No indexed £9,000 uplift, air-to-air, planning/noise, listed-building, or oil running-cost content.
- **Weaknesses:** ASHP buried in deep URL hierarchy; area pages are generic plumbing pages, not heat-pump-specific; 30-day guarantee headline undercuts trust.
- **Location targeting:** The most systematic of the local players: town-level area pages across four counties — but for plumbing generally, not heat pumps.

### Puzzle Plumbers — puzzleplumbers.com
- **Structure:** Town-template model: `/{town}/air-source-heat-pumps` replicated across Aylesbury, Milton Keynes, Watford, Kempston, Knebworth etc.; also a sister brand (renewableplumbers.com). Grant figure in the page title ("£7.5k grant").
- **Trust signals:** Family-run since 2010 (named founder), MCS certified, Mitsubishi Ecodan approved installer, customer review snippets, performance claims (≈4× efficiency, "up to 30%" bill savings).
- **Content coverage:** Best local BUS messaging — explains they check eligibility and apply on the customer's behalf with the £7,500 deducted upfront. **Still says £7,500 — no £9,000 uplift.** Nothing on air-to-air, planning/noise, listed buildings, or oil comparisons.
- **Weaknesses:** Hard-coding "£7.5k" into titles/URLs means their pages go stale on 21 July 2026. Template pages are near-identical across towns — no genuine local substance. No visible review-platform aggregate score.
- **Location targeting:** Aggressive town-page templating over a wide Beds/Bucks/Herts radius.

### Checkatrade — checkatrade.com (Q2 #3)
- **Structure:** Standard marketplace results page: 14 vetted ASHP traders for Aylesbury with per-trader review counts and vetting badges, plus request-a-quote flow.
- **Trust signals:** Platform-level vetting + per-trader review scores (the strongest social proof in the SERP).
- **Weaknesses/opportunity:** Zero educational or grant content; purely transactional. Also a *channel*: being listed there with strong reviews captures buyers we can't reach organically.

### Air To Heat — airtoheat.co.uk
- **Structure:** Specialist ASHP site: town template pages (`/air-pump-heat-installation/{town}/` — Aylesbury, Fleet, etc.), a "Why us" page; process-led copy (typical 2-day install).
- **Trust signals:** MCS, TrustMark, NICEIC accredited; 60+ years combined team experience; 7-year warranty on systems; 25 Trustpilot reviews (low volume but real); performance target of ~400% SCOP; savings claims of 25–40%.
- **Content coverage:** £7,500 grant messaging. No £9,000 uplift, air-to-air, planning/noise, listed-building, or oil-comparison content indexed. No prominent case studies.
- **Weaknesses:** Small review base; wide-radius templating (Aylesbury and Fleet, Hampshire on the same template) signals thin localisation.
- **Location targeting:** Programmatic town pages over a very wide area.

### Glow Green — glowgreenltd.com (Q3 #5)
- **Structure:** National installer with a "help and advice" content hub; the ranking page is a BUS eligibility explainer (what the scheme is, who qualifies, EPC requirement, installer-led application) with related grant articles (heat pump grants, "can you get a grant for air conditioning").
- **Trust signals:** National brand scale; content hub authority.
- **Content coverage:** Good BUS eligibility walkthrough (£7,500, EPC, ownership, fossil-fuel replacement, installer-led process). ⚠ £9,000 uplift coverage not confirmed in index at time of research.
- **Why it matters:** Proves a commercial "BUS explained + we're the installer" page ranks alongside Ofgem/GOV.UK. Nobody does this with Buckinghamshire localisation.

### Heater Grants — heatergrants.co.uk (Q4 #4)
- **Structure:** Lead-capture landing page: "free heat pump" framing, eligibility checklist gated on means-tested benefits (ECO4 route, not BUS), form-first design.
- **Trust signals:** Minimal; scheme-name authority borrowing.
- **Weaknesses:** Conflates ECO4 and BUS; only serves benefits-qualifying households; thin content. Ranks on aggressive keyword targeting — an honest, clearer page explaining *both* routes (ECO4 vs BUS £7,500/£9,000) would out-serve the query.

### Institutional pages (Ofgem, GOV.UK, MCS, Energy Saving Trust)
Unbeatable head-on, but they create the opportunity: they explain the scheme nationally and then tell users to "find an MCS installer near you". None mention Buckinghamshire specifics, none give costs vs oil for a real house, and their £9,000 uplift pages are dry policy statements. A local page that *completes the journey* those pages start (scheme → eligibility → named local MCS installer → survey booking) fills the gap they deliberately leave.

## 4. Prioritised content gaps to exploit

Ranked by (a) urgency, (b) commercial intent, (c) absence of local competition.

### P1 — £9,000 oil/LPG grant uplift page (URGENT: uplift goes live 21 July 2026 — ~2 weeks away)
Announced 21 April 2026; £9,000 for off-gas-grid homes replacing oil or LPG boilers with an air-source or ground-source heat pump; runs until 31 March 2027; installer-led via MCS. Not one local competitor mentions it, and Puzzle Plumbers have "£7.5k" baked into their page titles. Target: "£9,000 heat pump grant Buckinghamshire", "oil boiler replacement grant [village]". Angle: the Vale of Aylesbury and Chilterns villages are heavily off-gas-grid — name the villages. Publish *before* 21 July to own the news window Aira is currently winning nationally.

### P2 — Running costs vs oil: a Buckinghamshire worked example
National data: oil ≈ £825–£1,300+/yr fuel (55–65p/litre, 1,500–2,000 L, no price-cap protection); heat pumps 3–4× efficient, typically 30–50% cheaper to run than oil; payback for oil/LPG homes ≈ 2–4 years once the £9,000 grant applies. Nobody localises this. Build a worked example for a typical 4-bed detached oil-heated Bucks village house, and pair with P1 as the conversion engine.

### P3 — Planning permission & noise rules guide, localised
The 1-metre boundary rule was scrapped in England (GPDO amendment, in force 29 May 2025); permitted development now hinges on the MCS 020/MCS 020(a) noise assessment (⚠ verify exact current dB threshold — sources cite the long-standing 42 dB(A) figure and a newer 37 dB(A) assessment-position figure), unit volume ≤ 1.5 m³, and up to two units on detached houses. Zero local coverage. Localise with Buckinghamshire Council planning context and Chilterns National Landscape considerations. High-anxiety, pre-purchase question = strong lead magnet.

### P4 — Heat pumps in listed buildings & conservation areas
Completely uncovered by every competitor, institutional pages included — and Bucks is dense with listed cottages, conservation-area villages, and the Chilterns National Landscape. Cover: when listed building consent is needed, siting/screening strategies, high-temperature heat pumps for hard-to-insulate stock, and that grant eligibility still applies. This is a defensible, locally-differentiated asset no template site can copy.

### P5 — Air-to-air heat pump grant explainer (£2,500)
The BUS added £2,500 for air-to-air heat pumps (residential only; full removal of the fossil-fuel system required; no boiler kept as backup). Coverage is thin and *contradictory* — some 2026 blogs claim air-to-air now qualifies for £7,500 (⚠ verify against GOV.UK/Ofgem before publishing; official pages state £2,500). An accurate air-to-water (£7,500/£9,000) vs air-to-air (£2,500) comparison page would rank on confusion alone and up-sell readers to the bigger-grant option.

### P6 — "Boiler Upgrade Scheme installer in Buckinghamshire" service page
Q3's SERP is four institutional pages + one national installer. Mirror what makes Glow Green rank (eligibility checklist, EPC requirement, installer-led application walkthrough, "we apply for you") but localised, with our MCS number, and internal-linked from P1/P5. Also out-position Heater Grants by honestly explaining ECO4 (benefits-gated, potentially free) vs BUS (open to owner-occupiers and landlords) in one place.

### P7 — Trust-asset programme (site-wide, not a page)
The bar locally: MCS + manufacturer accreditations (Green Mechanical), 1,000+ reviews claimed (Red Van), 7-year warranties (Air To Heat, Samsung via Green Mechanical), maintenance plans (AOS). To beat it: display MCS certificate number in the header/footer; seed Trustpilot *and* Checkatrade (14 Aylesbury listings = a channel, not just a rival); publish named-village case studies with photos, heat-loss numbers, and before/after running costs (no competitor publishes real case studies); lead with a multi-year workmanship guarantee to contrast Red Van's 30 days.

## 5. Location-targeting patterns observed (and the gap)

Three models in play:
1. **County/district landing pages** — AOS (`/buckinghamshire`, `/aylesbury-vale`). Broad, shallow, brand-diluted.
2. **Programmatic town templates** — Puzzle, Air To Heat, Red Van's `/areas/` tree. Wide net, near-duplicate content, no local substance.
3. **Directories** — place-name pages at national scale (Renewable Energy Hub, Checkatrade).

Nobody does **model 4: genuinely local content** — off-gas-grid village targeting (highest £9,000-grant intent), housing-stock-specific guidance (1930s semis in Aylesbury vs listed Chilterns cottages), local planning authority references, or named local case studies. That is the differentiator to build; a modest number of *real* local pages should beat any volume of templates.

## 6. Key policy facts referenced (for our content team)

- BUS grant: £7,500 (air-source or ground-source), England & Wales; installer-led application via Ofgem; MCS installer required; EPC required with no outstanding loft/cavity insulation recommendations; full replacement of the fossil-fuel/electric system.
- **Uplift: £9,000 for oil/LPG (off-gas-grid) homes, announced 21 April 2026, effective 21 July 2026, expected to run to 31 March 2027.** Gas-boiler replacements stay at £7,500.
- Air-to-air heat pumps: £2,500 (residential only). Biomass: £5,000. ⚠ Some third-party sources claim air-to-air parity at £7,500 from April 2026 — verify on GOV.UK before citing.
- Permitted development (England): 1 m boundary rule removed 29 May 2025; MCS 020 noise assessment governs (42 dB(A) legacy limit; newer 37 dB(A) assessment-position figure cited — ⚠ verify); unit volume ≤ 1.5 m³ (house) / 0.6 m³ (flats); up to two units permitted on detached homes.
- Running costs: oil ≈ £825–£1,300+/yr fuel; heat pump efficiency 300–400%; typical saving vs oil 30–50% (≈ £150–£300+/yr conservatively); payback 2–4 yrs for oil/LPG homes under the £9,000 grant.
